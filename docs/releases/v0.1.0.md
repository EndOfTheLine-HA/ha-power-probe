# v0.1.0: How much power does that use?

Initial public release of Power Probe for Home Assistant. It needs Home Assistant
2026.10.2 or newer.

## Included

- HACS custom-repository installation and UI configuration. One entry holds the
  default whole-home power sensor, timing and cycles; the sensor can be changed for
  any single run.
- A timed off, on, off measurement against any whole-home power sensor in watts or
  kilowatts, on any switch, light, fan, input_boolean, humidifier, media_player,
  remote or climate entity.
- A result with an uncertainty and a quality rating (good, fair, poor or
  inconclusive) that says what limited it: other loads changing, a noisy meter, or
  repeated cycles disagreeing.
- Fast, medium and slow timing presets or custom seconds, and one to five cycles.
  The uncertainty counts distinct meter updates, not how often Power Probe looks.
- An admin-only sidebar panel with live progress, cancel, saved results and a
  ready-made Powercalc fixed-power snippet.
- The `power_probe.start_measurement` action (it returns the result when called
  with a response variable), `power_probe.cancel_measurement`, a
  `power_probe_finished` event, a status sensor and a last-result sensor.
- The device is put back the way it was after a finished, failed or cancelled run,
  and after a Home Assistant restart in the middle of a run.

## Verified scope

Automated tests cover the measurement arithmetic against a simulated house, real
config and options flows, actions, sensors, websocket commands and restart recovery
(with deliberate-break checks on the recovery code), plus repository checks. See the
repository's Actions checks for the tagged revision.

Checked by hand in a real Home Assistant 2026.10.2: the panel, a full run, and
recovery after a hard kill in the middle of a run. Used in one real house with a
Refoss EM16P meter on two devices. Both results rated Poor and came within about 1.2
times their stated uncertainty of the reference. The README's accuracy section has
the numbers.

## Important limits

- One short run is good to roughly plus or minus 60 W in a house with cycling loads
  the meter does not see. That suits loads of about 500 W and up, or use several
  cycles and treat the number as an estimate.
- The meter needs to report at least three times in each reading window. A sensor
  that updates every two minutes is too slow for a normal run.
- It switches the device off and on. Do not use it on anything that must stay
  running, and stay nearby when testing a heater.
- It measures one operating point: a light at the brightness it turns on to, a fan
  at its current speed, and the extra draw compared with off.
- Tested in one house on one kind of meter. Not yet tried on Sense or Shelly meters.
